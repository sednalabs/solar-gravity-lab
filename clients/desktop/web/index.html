FLIGHT GUIDE

A small burn. A different orbit.

The main engine pushes along the nose. The camera moves independently. Bring the direction marker to the crosshair before adding throttle; a hollow marker means the target is behind you.

Shape the orbit

Prograde follows velocity. Near periapsis, a short burn mainly raises the opposite apoapsis. Retrograde opposes velocity; near apoapsis, a short burn mainly lowers periapsis.

Normal / anti-normal tilt the orbital plane. Radial out / in point away from or towards the host, changing the orbit's shape and orientation.

Feel the spacecraft

W A S D pitch and yaw, Q E roll. Switch to RCS for translation. Fine reduces control authority to 20%. Releasing a key stops the input, not existing motion.

Stabilise damps rotation. + / − adjusts throttle, X cuts all manual thrust, Space pauses. Lost focus clears controls and pauses flight.

Plan, try, learn

Pause and preview a finite burn. The native solver begins at your current attitude and applies thrust while the ship turns. Mark a checkpoint before trying it; Reset opens a new timeline at that moment. Periapsis and apoapsis are instantaneous estimates, and no atmospheric drag or terrain collision is modelled.

Open a spacecraft definition or scenario to try a new vehicle. Opening a new world first downloads a backup of the current flight. Workshop demonstrates two data-defined spacecraft with different engines.

Coast accuracy

Powered flight uses the forward-time method. Change the coast method for a specific accuracy need; higher cost is not automatically better.
